## 💻 Development projects

### Shelf Help
A personal project, item and notes manager that runs as a self-hosted Home Assistant add-on.

It keeps track of hobby and household projects (active ones and "someday" ideas), the tools and parts they need, where those things are kept, and the notes that go with them. Claude can also read and update it through a built-in MCP server.

Developed with Python (FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, SQLite) and React

### Ohm Sweet Ohm
A self-hosted electronics workbench for hobby builds made from off-the-shelf modules (ESP32 and Arduino boards, sensor breakouts, displays, relays). You keep a library of parts with their real pinouts, wire them together on a canvas, and the server checks the circuit for mistakes before you power anything up. From the same design it lays the circuit out on stripboard and generates firmware scaffolding.

Claude is built in as a collaborator over MCP: it can read and review designs, build new ones, add parts from datasheets and lay circuits out on a board. It works on the same data as the UI, under the same rules.

It runs as a Home Assistant add-on and sits alongside its sibling app, Shelf-help, which tracks component stock and projects.

Developed with Python (FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, SQLite) and React

### 🎮 3D design / electronic projects

- Designed a fully custom H-pattern shifter in Fusion 360 for 3D printing for a friend; The goal was to make something that would feel mechanical, but still use 3D printing. The mechanical feel was achieved by pulling and pushing bearings held down by spring tension over a patterned indexer to add some resistance. 
- Auto Vacuum switch; I wanted to make something that would automatically turn on my workshop vacuum when a power tool is turned on. I designed and 3D printed an enclosure to house a microcontroller, AC to DC power supply, current sensor and a relay. It powers on automatically when current is detected and switches off 6 seconds later once no current is detected.
- Life sized Thunder hammer from the Warhammer universe; This was an impulse project. After playing Space Marine 2, I thought it would be a good idea to 3D print a weapon from the game to serve as a display piece in my workshop. More than 100 hours of 3D printing, 5.5KG's worth of filament, 2 weeks of sanding and another week of experimenting with paint.
